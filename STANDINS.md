# Stand-ins

Author of the box: Nobody, allegedly. Written by the builder of `claude/agreements-c-2`.

The Agreements text leans on layers that are not in the box. Its section 8.1 says what it needs from them and does not have; its section 9 says what a builder needs that is not in the text. Each stand-in below is the simplest thing that let the build go on. Each sits behind one small interface, named in the last column, so that it can be replaced without touching the grammar rules. None of them is a proposal for the real layer.

A sentence in quotation marks is the sentence of section 8.1 or section 9 the stand-in stands in for.

| # | Stand-in | What was chosen, and why | Stands in for | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | **The act, as bytes** | A text record: a first line `mor-act 1 <type>`, then lines of a key and tokens (printable ASCII, one space apart), then `sig <64 hex>` for acts, then `end`. Field names are the ones of section 3. A file is a run of records. Chosen because it can be written and read by hand, and a strict line format is easy to decode safely and to fuzz. | 9.1: "The act, as bytes. Terms and Statement written as acts of the core: how `agreement`, `replaces` and `previous` are carried (the seams' A1), how `for` and `under` are (A4)". 8.1: "The core's act format: whether it already gives fingerprints, a per-signer line of acts (which would make G6 redundant), acts signed by several people at once, and field names." | `src/decode.c` (one entry point, `decode_all`); the writer is `mor_seal` in `src/standin.c` |
| S2 | **The fingerprint** | SHA-256 over the record's bytes, from `mor-act` through `end\n`, written as 64 lower-case hex characters. One hash for acts, cMIPs, words, envelopes and settlements alike. A token is "named by fingerprint" when it has that shape. | 9.1: "what each "fingerprint" of this text is a fingerprint of: an act, a cMIP, a signer or a work, which the core names apart." 8.1: "How cMIPs are identified in practice, so that "named by fingerprint" in G2 can be met." | `mor_options.fingerprint` (default `mor_standin_fingerprint`, `src/standin.c`) |
| S3 | **The signer and the signature** | A signer is an identifier that starts with `@` and never changes. The signature is a keyless tag: SHA-256 over a fixed prefix and the record's bytes before the `sig` line. Anyone can compute it: **it proves nothing.** It keeps the shape "an act is signed and a verifier checks it"; an act whose tag does not check is rejected. One act has one signer. A key that is replaced or lost does not exist here. | 9.2: "The signer. In the core a signer is an identity that outlives its keys. This text says "signer" and does not say what a change of key does to a line (G6)". 8.1: "What the Sign layer does when a key is replaced or lost, since that decides whether G6 sees a fork." and "Whether the core has an act signed by several people at once". | `mor_options.sig_check` (default `mor_standin_sig_check`, `src/standin.c`) |
| S4 | **The place** | A record `place` signed by the place: its identifier, and entries `entry <position> <fingerprint>`: a signed list of the acts it was shown, with positions. An act is "shown by the place" when an answer held lists it. Several answers of one place are put together; the same act at two positions, or two acts at one position, is a contradiction and gives no answer (G7.2). No answer held from a named place gives no answer either (G9.1). "Nothing later" is read as of the answers held. | 9.4: "The place. Nothing below gives one." 8.1: "A place, from the layer that orders acts [...] (2) one line of the acts the place was shown, in the order it was shown them; (3) a signed answer, by the place, that says of an act [...] whether it is in that line and at which position; [...] (5) a way for a verifier to tell that two answers of one place contradict each other". | `place_get`, `place_pos`, `view_of`, `visible` in `src/engine.c` |
| S5 | **The clock** | A record `clock`: its identifier and one number, `time <n>`. Two answers of one clock that differ give no answer. Only a stub cMIP reads it (`after`). | 9.5: "The clock. [...] How `clock` holds one, and what a verifier answers for an act nobody anchored, is not said." 8.1: "What a clock is, which layer gives it, and how it is identified without a server address (G7.4)." | `clock_time` in `src/engine.c` |
| S6 | **A cMIP in a socket, and the stub cMIPs** | A cMIP is a document: a record `cmip` with one line `stub <behaviour>`, named by its fingerprint. A socket is `<fingerprint> <parameters...>`. Each of the eight tasks has one function that is handed what its row of section 5 says and gives back that row's output, or "cannot apply" (the document is not held, or its behaviour is unknown): then the grammar answers **unchecked**. The behaviours: `yes`, `no` (every socket: always yes, always no); *absent* (the document is not held); K2 `count N who...` (N of those listed have assented; an agreement's root may be listed); K3 `signer who...` (the act's signer is listed), `author` (the act's signer is an author: shows that `authors` is handed in, G3.2); K4 `count N who...` (the signature-counting stub section 9.3 asks for, two of three), `after T` (valid from time T, per the clock); K5 `witness W one\|many [max N]` (a filling for each Statement by the witness W whose `content` is `fills <role> <signer>`; the proof is that Statement; the version earned under is its `terms`; still open while fewer than N); K1 `plain` (see S10); K6 `yes`, `no`; K7 any document held is the kind's definition; K8 `yes`, `no`, `readers who...`. | 9.3: "A cMIP in a socket. How a cMIP is named with its parameters, what exactly each socket hands it and gets back as data, and a few stub cMIPs that every builder shares (always yes, always no, absent). From draft 19 the update condition's cMIP also answers who must sign, so its stubs need one that counts signatures (two of three)". 9.12: "The data cMIP. [...] a stub that every builder shares". | `src/stubs.c`: `k1_read`, `k2_in_force`, `k3_power`, `k4_condition`, `k5_spot`, `k6_control`, `k7_kind`, `k8_data` |
| S7 | **The report and the call** | A call is a Terms line `call <task> <doer\|receiving\|both>`. A report is a record of its own type `report` (not a Statement, in no signer's line): its signer; `call <fingerprint>`: the act that holds the call it answers (the Terms version, or for a buyer's confirmation the payment or its settlement); `relies_on`; and the lines of K6's row: `received <amount> <unit> <settlement>`, `passed <amount> <unit> <to> <settlement>`, `own`, `kept`, `owed <amount> <unit> <to>`. A report with no `call` is rejected: "A report that names no call is no report" (the block's R2). A report with any line other than `received` is read as a doer's; one with only `received` lines as a receiving side's. | 9.6: "The report, written into the shared language. One act type, defined once: how a call is written and where it sits, how a report names its call [...]. Until then a report has no fields." 8.1: "What the block has not yet said: how a call is written, where it sits in the act that holds the task, and how a report names it by fingerprint". 8.1, the seam: "that a report may name the call it answers by naming the act that holds it (here the payment, by its settlement)". | decoded in `src/decode.c`; read in `leg_reports`, `tie_line`, `payment_one` in `src/report.c` |
| S8 | **Money's side: payment, settlement, amount, "the same payment"** | A `payment` record: signer (the payer), `to <root>`, optionally `version <fingerprint>` (the version it read), `settlement <fingerprint>`, `amount <whole number> <unit>`. A `settlement` record is opaque: its signer (who gave it) and `payment <id>`. Two settlements are "of the same payment" when they carry the same id: that is the stand-in for Money's answer where it records one payment as two acts. An amount is a whole number and a unit, compared exactly. A payment "pending" does not exist. | 9.7: "Money's side. What a payment and a settlement are as acts (a claim, a receipt), how a payment names the version it read, the answer to "the same payment", how an amount is written, where the payer's default call sits, and what a verifier answers while a payment is pending." 8.1: ""The same payment", and Money's counting." and "How that layer writes an amount". | `same_settlement`, `payment_one`, `amount_str` in `src/report.c` |
| S9 | **The bake** | An `envelope` record: `baker <signer>` and `work <fingerprint>`. Two envelopes carry the same work when their `work` is the same: that stands in for the core's answer about their bytes. | 9.8: "The bake. What this text calls an envelope the core calls a bake, and its baker an uploader; and how two bakes are known to carry the same work (G14.5)." 8.1: "What the core calls an envelope, baking and a baker (G14): whether an envelope has a fingerprint of its own, how it states who baked it, and how a verifier tells that two envelopes carry the same work". | `baker_rec`, `same_work_note` in `src/report.c` |
| S10 | **The words of a body, and its parts for machines** | A `words` record: free `text` lines, and `line` lines that the body stub `plain` reads as parts for machines: `line share <party> <n>` (the part each party is given), `line delegation <delegator> <agent>`, `line end none\|agent-only\|other` (the conditions on ending a delegation), `line passes <signer>` (to whom these Terms pass the baker's rights: the text defines no act for that, P18). Where the words or the body cMIP are not held the body cannot be read. | 9.10: "The words of a body. How the fixed words of Terms are held, and the shape of the "parts for machines" the body task gives: shares, and what makes Terms a delegation (K1)." | `k1_read` in `src/stubs.c` |
| S11 | **Who is reading, and which anchoring the reader accepts** | Options of the library and of the tool: `reader` (a signer identifier, or none: "a verifier told nothing reads as no side of any leg"), `anchoring_place` and `anchoring_clock` (used only where an agreement names none, G13.4). | 9.11: "Who is reading. A verifier must be told on whose behalf it reads, and which anchoring that reader accepts (G11.4(d), G13.4). No layer says how." | `mor_options` in `include/mor.h`; `--reader`, `--anchoring-place`, `--anchoring-clock` in `tools/morv.c` |
| S12 | **How a role is named** | A role in `parties` carries a local label: `party role <label> kind=... what=... cmip=... <parameters>`. `authors` and `receiver` name it as `role:<label>`. The text says a role may be an author or a receiver and gives no way to point at one. | 9.1 (field names) and section 3.1's rows for `authors` and `receiver`: "one or more of the parties named as signers or roles"; "one of the parties, named as a signer or a role". | `find_role` in `src/engine.c`; decoded in `line_terms` |
| S13 | **Delivery** | Nothing was built: the grammar does not read it. A buyer's contest is the buyer's word. | 9.9: "Delivery. What shows that what was bought was handed over, or acknowledged (G11.7, G11.8)." | none |
| S14 | **The answer words** | This text's words only (shown, not shown, fork, valid, not valid, invalid, unchecked), the block's (matched, mismatched, one side only, unanswered, no report called for), and "rejected". The core's "unknown", "void" and "pending" have no word here. Two outputs of tasks are passed on as "yes" and "no" (K8; K5's "still takes new identities"). | 9.13: "The answer words. This text's answers beside the core's (unknown, void, pending), so that a builder of both layers shows one vocabulary". 8.1: "Whether the verifier's answers [...] are already named in another layer". | `mor_answer` in `include/mor.h` |
| S15 | **Where a verifier stops** | Three numbers, options of the library: powers through powers, 8 levels; receivers from agreement to agreement, 8; versions of one chain, 1024. Beyond them the answer is unchecked, with how far it followed (G9.2). A fourth number is fixed in the code: the readings spent on counter signatures that lean on one another (64 for one counter signature, 100 000 for a run); fuzzing showed that without it a set of acts can make a verifier work without end. | Section 6, P2 and P8: "The number is not fixed". | `mor_options.max_power_depth`, `max_receiver_hops`, `max_chain`; `MOR_MAX_COUNTER_WORK` in `src/internal.h` |
| S16 | **Drafts** | A convenience of the stand-in format, used by the tests and the tool: records written without `sig` and `end`, a line `=name` to label the next record, `$name` for its fingerprint in later records, `~name` for a made-up fingerprint of something not held. Not part of any layer. | nothing in the text: it exists because S1 and S2 make acts name each other by hash | `mor_draft` in `src/draft.c`; `morv seal` |

Not stood in for, because the grammar does not need it to give its answers: the lower texts' own words (9.14), what a client is (8.1; G12.8 is a duty of clients), and the research's open question on what a proven liar stands to lose (8.1).
