# MOR Freeze Test Suite

*Version 30, freeze-ready, for Nobody, allegedly, to approve.*

*This suite tests the six freeze-ready MIRs: Identity draft 15 (its "Written for people" section included), Text draft 11, Envelopes draft 11, Money draft 11, Agreements draft 17 and Development draft 11, mapped by MICS v30. A rule is cited by its MIR's name and number ("Agreements rule 76"); a step is cited by scenario and number ("step 3.7ab"). Where a step and a MIR differ, the MIR decides.*

## Purpose

This is the end-game checklist for freezing the MOR core. It is for the founding team, not for readers of the protocol.

**Freeze rule:** the MICS is frozen whole, its six MIRs together, at the first release. Before that, each scenario below is either **run**, exercised against the founding components and passed, or **reasoned**, walked through on paper against the texts without running code. Every scenario the founding components can run is run. The freeze publishes, with the six texts and their hashes, a **freeze report** marking each scenario and each core component run or reasoned. The cMIPs and Modules used may be clunky; only the core has to hold. A failure found before the freeze that needs a core change is a finding, fixed before the freeze.

*A reasoned rule is frozen on argument, not evidence. The report says so, so nobody mistakes one for the other, and the next reviewer knows where to press first.*

**Conformance versus validity.** Some pass criteria name rules only the signer's own client can honour (what you sign is what you saw; showing what a rule computes; never defaulting to a device-bound policy; keepers never holding content keys). They are tested by inspecting the founding clients, not by verifiers, and are marked *(conformance)*.

### 4. A democracy round

1. Eligible identities prove membership through a verifying module, without revealing who they are.
2. Each allocates or delegates a fixed pool of points.
3. Ballots are sealed containers, published locked on a relay, mirrored, and committed by hash at round close; the keys are published when the round closes. *Ballots are not signed acts, since every act names a signer; the voting cMIP defines their form. The commitment is the voting cMIP's own: the core holds no relay commitment.*
4. Anyone recomputes the result from a mirror.

**Passes if:** points behave as a unit under conservation, no voter is linkable to a ballot, no relay could read a ballot before the close, and every mirror matches the commitment.

7c. A second, single-home identity of the journalist loses its home when the operator closes it by rotation. The journalist leaves with a homeless rotation; declared auditors attest absence where a home vanished without closing, after it had receipted an audited rotation; the journalist's client carries that home's inclusion proofs to the new home, so a reader who never reached the old home still proves the history. A thief's homeless rotation for a live home is voided by that home's objection. A live but hostile home refuses the journalist's normal rotation; the journalist leaves with both keys, the endorsement listing the refused rotation as abandoned. The journalist's declared auditor closes; the journalist rotates, dropping the requirement, and the rotation counts on the home's receipt alone. After re-homing, the journalist rotates once more; a late objection changes nothing. A thief who finds the journalist's long-used first chain key on an old backup, after that home has closed, makes a homeless rotation at that old position and rotates again at once; the rotations the home receipted still count, and the thief's chain counts for nothing. A thief holding the chain key of an audited identity drops auditing in a homeless rotation, and names an auditor of its own; neither a reader's failed attempt nor the thief's auditor makes the live home gone. A censored reader: a third single-home identity of the journalist, with no auditors, has its home abroad. A thief holding its chain key makes a homeless rotation naming the thief's own homes, and a censor blocks the real home from the reader's network. The reader's client tries every known address and asks relays to probe, all blocked; it shows the identity as re-homed without audit, and sends it nothing private without a plain warning *(conformance)*. The thief rotates again at once under the homes it chose; the first rotation is not made final. The home's objection later reaches the reader in a bundle: the homeless rotation and the thief's second rotation are both void, and the identity's last counting act is again the one before the theft. A thief with the signing key redirects the inbox and the encryption key; deliveries in the window are re-sent after rotation.
7d. A thief holding the journalist's chain key gets a genuine rival rotation receipted by a home whose stolen operator key also receipts the journalist's. The identity is contested at that position until the operator rotates; the audit settles which receipt is real; the home is proven dishonest at that position, the journalist's earlier rotations keep counting, and the journalist's earlier receipt under a cosigned summary survives.
7e. **Evidence its signer disowned.** A thief holding a home operator's signing key signs an objection to the journalist's homeless rotation of step 7c; an accomplice signs a witness act naming it; the operator rotates and disowns the objection: the objection is shown as a dispute, voids nothing, and the exit counts. The same for a cosignature of a forged log summary that the auditor's rotation disowns: it protects no receipt; and for a witness act its signer's rotation disowns: the post it named is void, not disputed. Before the operator rotates, the objection voids the homeless rotation; the journalist, holding both keys, leaves at once by the escape, endorsed with the current signing key: a stated cost.
7f. **A contested self-hosted position.** The journalist's self-hosted identity of step 6 meets a thief's rotation, signed with its stolen chain key, at the same position as the owner's: contested for good, nobody wins. The air-gapped Module refuses the escape on the online side's word alone; presented with the thief's rotation, it checks it offline (its signature verifies under the chain key it revealed, its previous act is the same, it is not the rotation the device signed) and signs the escape; the escape counts. A refusal by the homes that count (the first case of Identity rule 29's exception) stays unprovable from bytes: the Module tells the human what it is asked to sign, and why, before signing *(conformance)*.
7g. **A rotation that never counted.** The journalist's client changes the homes and leaves in place a home rule that no longer fits: the rotation is invalid, and no home stores it; the journalist signs again with the same chain key, and that rotation counts. Before signing again, the air-gapped Module accepts only bytes: given the identity chain from genesis to the first rotation's predecessor, it runs the rotation checks offline itself, finds the first rotation invalid, and signs; given the online side's report alone, it refuses.
8. The journalist declares a successor in a rotation, and links their MOR identity to an account on another protocol, which confirms by signing the claim in its own protocol. They sign in to an outside service with MOR after the rotation; a sign-in with the old key fails.
8b. **A succession confirmed.** A stranger's rotation declares the journalist its successor: every client shows it as declared, never followed. The successor the journalist declared in step 8 is followed once it signs a link confirmation naming the rotation, or, in a variant, a witness act naming it; until then it is shown as declared.
9. Throughout, a messaging client with no Money or Agreements exchanges text with both parties, including a text act with a format it lacks, shown as plain text. A client that has the format shows the same act formatted: every character it hides is markup, never a letter or digit, it adds none, and it shows the rest in the order of the bytes, readable; the plain text is one tap away.

**Passes if:** a reply's named signer leads a stranger to the post it answers while deciding nothing about validity, likes and replies keep nothing alive while a witness act keeps the promise as a dispute, no linkage exists until confirmed, the disclosed thread verifies as complete up to the last acknowledgement, no relay could read the private thread or name its sender, the two parties met without sharing a relay, the routine rotation loses no history, the non-storing home is outvoted, the thief's rotations lose, no identity is frozen or contested by a forged receipt or summary, no signing key can close a home, the homeless and both-keys exits work and become final, a homeless rotation accepted on a censored reader's own failed attempt is labelled and never made final by the thief's next rotation, and the objection undoes both however late it arrives, a used chain key found after a closure rewrites no history, the audit requirement is droppable, the self-hosted identity rotates, succession is readable by any client, and a formatted text reads the same words, in the same order, with the format as without it; evidence its signer's rotation disowned decides nothing (5.7e), a verifier lacking acts says unknown (5.5c), a contested self-hosted position is left by the escape the Module signs on bytes (5.7f), a rotation that never counted leaves the chain key free to sign again (5.7g), and no succession is followed unconfirmed (5.8b).

### 7. A subscription service

1. A music service signs agreements with creators naming its split cMIP: one creator group under pro-rata sharing, another under user-centric sharing. The service is their split service: each agreement is a deal whose terms carry each creator's grant to the service, naming "this agreement" by null, one per payee (field 14), allowing it to batch payouts monthly on a named time reference. Listeners pay the service's own pointer (step 2), so it signs those receipts with its own key; a creator's grant key signs only receipts for money reaching that creator's pointer under the deal, never the service's payouts to the creator.
2. A listener subscribes: an agreement with a monthly obligation against the time reference, signed by the listener, paid to the service's payee pointer.
3. Keys for new content are released through a delegated key-release service while payments are discharged.
4. A play-count module reports listening, with its own signed use record; each month, every incoming receipt is matched by a split under each creator's chosen model, and every payout is paid with its own receipt. One batched payout proof legitimately covers several payouts whose amounts sum to it.
5. The listener's payment lapses: the obligation shows as past its terms, and no new keys are released.
6. The listener cancels; past access stands.
7. One month the service publishes no split for part of what it received; a listener's claim shows a payment the service never receipted: the unmatched receipts and claims show as its open obligations. Each creator revokes its own grant, by an everyday act, public; the parties clone their deals, every party signing, field 14 listing grants to a new split service, and each creator publishes a new payee pointer version; the old service still owes what it received.
8. **The pool layer.** The service's pool is the first of the layers money passes through: one creator's song is carried by a publisher, under a publication's agreement whose parties are the song's own agreement and the publisher, each naming its own split service. The pool's split names the publication's split service as the receiver of the publication's share; that service receipts it in its own name and splits it under the publication's agreement, naming the work's split service as the receiver of the work's share; that service receipts in its own name and splits under the work's agreement. A payout of the work's share to one of the work's payees directly, or to anyone else, is a mismatch. Where a rail pays a creator's own account, the work's service signs for that share on the creator's behalf with the creator's grant key, the rail's proof on the receipt: it counts; the same receipt without the rail's proof counts for nothing, and so does that key signing a payout of the work's service's own split, proof or not.
8a. **The pointer the work's agreement designates.** The publication's split pays the work's agreement's share to the one pointer that agreement designates for payments, the same whoever pays, and divides nothing: a work whose field 14 names a split service and whose split cMIP's fields name another as its backup (field 29) is paid at field 14's, a payout to the backup a mismatch, unless, for that payment, the attempt at field 14's failed as the split cMIP defines failure: then the backup is paid, and the next payment is tried at field 14's again; a payout to anyone the owners did not sign is a mismatch whatever is stated; a work whose payees grant two different services designates none, a payout to either a mismatch; a lone owner's work (one party, every stake hers) is paid at her own pointer, nothing to split; a work of two owners naming one of them as its split service (an owner acting as treasury) is paid to that owner, a payout to the other a mismatch; a work of two owners naming no split service cannot be paid through the layer, a payout to either a mismatch. The layer above never divides the work's share.
8b. **A payment split exactly, each cut at its flow address.** A fan buys the publication for 1,000 units. The publication's split service tries the work's designated service S; S gives no payment request within the wait the split cMIP's fields set, so the attempt failed, and the work's share of 850 goes to its backup T, the label's 150 to the label. T receipts the share in its own name and splits it under the work's agreement, 510 to Ana and 340 to Ben, by the split cMIP. Each receipts its payout. Each participant's check (Agreements rule 18) finds its whole cut at its own flow address; the three cuts sum to the 1,000 paid; a cut whose receipt the rail places elsewhere is shown so; before any split, the cut is the service's open obligation. The next payment is tried at S again.

**Passes if:** both sharing models run side by side and each creator can see which applies to them, every monthly split balances, the lapse is verifiable against the time reference, access stops without any core change, and the failed month is visible and owed, including what only the payers' claims revealed; money passes layer by layer, each layer's split paying the next layer's split service, which receipts in its own name, or for an owner with the rail's proof, and splits under its own agreement, each receipt it has not split its open obligation.

## Freeze procedure

1. Fix what the texts fix only at the freeze: each frozen text's header becomes the text's name alone (for instance "# MIR: Identity", as Development rule 26 writes it), with no draft number or approval line, since the header is part of the hashed text; the SHA-256 of the X-Wing draft's revision 11, written into Envelopes as `XWING`; and then the six MIRs' spec hashes, computed as Development rule 26 says, with the MICS document's and this suite's. *The RV32IM profile and its vectors are not among them: the profile is a specification of its own, published after the freeze. The signature-scheme and unit specifications and the pinned Unicode version are written in the texts; how a home is queried is the relay transport cMIP's; the running-summary vectors over stand-in ids are published with the conformance vectors, the three-act sequence's final once the hashes are.*
2. Build the founding cMIPs and Modules: a home relay, a basic relay, the genesis client (with the air-gapped chain key Module), a read-only client and a basic client; and, so every layer meets friction, a rail Module (Money), a split Module and a deal-assessment tool (Agreements). Clunky is acceptable.
3. Run every scenario the founding components can run; reason through the rest against the texts. Mark each run or reasoned, ticking each component as it passes.
4. Machine adversarial review: the texts, the code and the draft freeze report, reviewed by a model given the budget to break things and propose fixes.
5. Specialist questions: from the draft freeze report, each rule that is only reasoned becomes a targeted question to a specialist in its field, one person and one question at a time. Then human adversarial review: one reviewer attacks the whole, the running prototypes as well as the texts. Nobody outside decides the freeze.
6. Fix any failure requiring a core change, and rerun or re-reason the affected scenarios.
7. When every box is ticked, run or reasoned, the MICS is frozen: the six MIRs' texts, committed as canonical text, their hashes and the freeze report are published together.

After the freeze, a flaw in the core is fixed only by a new protocol running alongside, with users migrating by choice.
